Why it matters

Google prints roughly 155 characters. Everything past that is thrown away, and the cut lands mid-word, so the sentence a searcher reads ends in the middle of a thought. Worse, none of the first 155 characters say where you are or what you sell. Somebody searching "pizza Duncan Ave" gets a paragraph about community values with no pizza and no address in it.

The fix

One tight sentence under 155 characters that leads with pizza, Pittsburgh and the thing that actually makes you different.

Why it matters

An H1 is meant to be the single most important line on the page. When sixteen lines all claim to be the most important one, none of them carry any weight, and search engines fall back on guessing. It also makes the page harder to navigate with a screen reader.

The fix

One H1 that says what the place is, and everything else demoted to H2 and H3. Purely structural, the page looks identical afterwards.

Why it matters

The placeholder files are the ones the website builder drops in before real photos get added, and three of them are still on the page. The oversized photo is roughly ten times bigger than the space it fills, which is loaded in full on every phone that visits. And half the images carry no description, so Google cannot tell that they are pizza.

The fix

Swap the placeholders for real photos of your pies, resize the big one, and label the rest.
